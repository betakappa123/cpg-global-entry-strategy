# Project Starting Point (`project-start.md`)

## Selected project area

**Consumer Strategy, Food and Agriculture — CPG Global Entry Strategy**

Our selected project area is consumer strategy in the coffee segment of the consumer packaged goods (CPG) industry. Coffee is a useful category for strategic analysis because it includes a broad range of products and consumption occasions, from whole beans, ground coffee, instant coffee, and coffee pods to ready-to-drink (RTD) coffee in bottles or cans. Consumer preferences, price points, shopping behavior, retail availability, and competitive conditions can vary significantly across products and markets. These differences create several meaningful decisions that can be investigated with market and consumer data.

Our team is considering three separate versions of the project:

1. **Whether to expand overseas** 
2. **Which retail channel to choose** 
3. **Which packaging strategy to adopt** 

Although the three versions address different business decisions, they belong to the same broader project area: using evidence to match a coffee product with the right market, route to the consumer, or packaging solution. We will compare the feasibility and value of the three versions before selecting the strongest direction for the final project.



## Why our team is interested in it

Our team is interested in this area because coffee is both an everyday consumer product and a category with complex strategic choices. The same coffee product can succeed or fail depending on where it is sold, how consumers discover and purchase it, and whether its packaging fits their needs. Packaged coffee, RTD coffee, and freshly brewed coffee serve different consumption occasions and require different approaches to pricing, distribution, promotion, and product presentation. This variety makes coffee a practical and engaging category through which to study consumer strategy.

We are also interested in the three project versions because each represents a real decision that a coffee company may face at a different stage of growth. The overseas-expansion version addresses growth potential and international risk. The retail-channel version focuses on customer access, brand visibility, and distribution economics. The packaging version connects consumer convenience and brand positioning with operational concerns such as cost, product protection, shelf life, and sustainability. A poor decision in any of these areas could limit demand, increase costs, or weaken the product's competitive position.

Most importantly, all three versions can turn available data into a clear and actionable recommendation. We can compare market size and growth, consumer spending, demographics, competitive or brand concentration, channel performance, product formats, and dependence on foreign suppliers. Our goal is not simply to describe the coffee market, but to use evidence to support a specific business decision. Comparing the three versions will help us choose a final project that is strategically meaningful, feasible with the available data, and relevant to both consumer needs and company performance.


## Candidate Vision A

**Whether to expand overseas**

We would evaluate whether a coffee company located in Yunnan Province, China should take its products into international markets. This version would compare domestic and overseas opportunities, identify promising countries, and assess market demand, growth, competition, purchasing power, import dependence, and the risks of international expansion.

## Candidate Vision B

**Which retail channel to choose** 

We would determine which retail channel offers the best route to target consumers in Asia-Pacific area, such as supermarkets, convenience stores, specialty retailers, cafés, or e-commerce platforms. This version would compare channel reach, shopper behavior, pricing, margins, brand visibility, and operational requirements.

## Candidate Vision C

**Which packaging strategy to adopt**

 We would recommend the most suitable packaging format for a coffee product. This version could compare bags, jars, pods, bottles, and cans, as well as package size, material, convenience, shelf life, transportation cost, sustainability, and fit with the intended brand position. It would also account for the different packaging needs of packaged coffee and RTD coffee.
