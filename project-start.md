# Project Starting Point (`project-start.md`)

## Selected project area
Global Consumer Market Opportunity (Consumer Strategy, Food and Agriculture)

## Why our team is interested in it
Our team is interested in understanding how global consumer goods brands navigate cross-border expansion in mature versus emerging markets. Evaluating international market viability requires balancing category size, disposable income, local brand concentration, and supply-chain dependency. This domain provides a rich context to apply data-driven decision frameworks to high-stakes corporate growth and supply decisions.

## Candidate Vision A
**VP of Global Expansion** at **Blue Bottle Coffee** must decide which two international country markets to enter for premium retail and ready-to-drink (RTD) distribution over the next three years.

## Candidate Vision B
**Chief Procurement Officer** at **JDE Peet's** must decide which agricultural origin markets to establish direct long-term supplier partnerships with to hedge against import-dependence shocks and rising tariffs.

## Core data source we confirmed we can access
**USC Passport / Euromonitor International**  
- **Link:** [USC Libraries Passport Database](https://libraries.usc.edu/databases/passport)  
- **Access confirmed:** Category market size (volume & value), 5-year forecast growth, per capita consumption, brand/company market share percentages, and country macroeconomic indicators.

## Three questions we still need to resolve
1. Does Passport provide category data granular enough (e.g., specialty/RTD coffee vs. aggregate hot drinks) across all target candidate countries?
2. How consistently are brand concentration and distribution channel splits (e-commerce vs. grocery vs. food service) reported across developing vs. developed markets?
3. Which auxiliary datasets (e.g., import tariffs, logistics performance index, or climate volatility metrics) will be necessary to substantiate the final decision?
